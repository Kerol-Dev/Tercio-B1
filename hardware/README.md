# Tercio B1 hardware — KiCad as code

The schematic is generated by Python and checked by KiCad 10 itself. **Do not
edit the files in `kicad/` by hand.** Change the code, regenerate, and review.

```bash
cd ~/Documents/Projects/Tercio-B1 && python3 hardware/build.py
```

```bash
cd ~/Documents/Projects/Tercio-B1 && python3 -I hardware/verify_bom.py
```

`build.py` writes the KiCad project and then:

1. exports the netlist with `kicad-cli` and compares every net, pin by pin,
   with the circuit the code describes (a wire that lands on the wrong pin, or
   two nets that touch, fails the build);
2. runs ERC at all severities;
3. exports `build/tercio-b1-schematic.pdf`.

Then the board, generated with KiCad's own Python (it needs `pcbnew`):

```bash
cd ~/Documents/Projects/Tercio-B1 && /Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/3.9/bin/python3 hardware/layout.py
```

`layout.py` loads every footprint from the KiCad netlist (reference, value, fields
and schematic path, so DRC can check schematic parity), places the fixed parts from
`b1/board.py`, auto-places the rest (courtyard- and hole-aware, per side; each part lands
on the line from the pin it serves toward where that signal goes next), draws the outline,
the power copper and planes from `b1/copper.py` and the art from `b1/art.py`, sets the
matte-black ENIG stackup and the net classes, then **routes the board**, adds the fills
(GND on F/In2/In3/B, +3V3 on In3 under the logic half), fills the zones and renders
`build/render-*.png`. `--no-route` stops after placement; `--route-only` routes the board
as placed.

```bash
cd ~/Documents/Projects/Tercio-B1/hardware && /Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/3.9/bin/python3 layout.py --no-route && FASTROUTE_ROUNDS=30 /Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/3.9/bin/python3 layout.py --route-only
```

### Routing

`gen/route_export.py` (KiCad's Python) turns the board and the plan in `b1/routing.py` into
flat arrays (`build/route/problem.npz` + `.json`); `gen/fastroute.py` solves them in a
separate Python 3.12 with numpy and Numba (`TERCIO_FAST_PY`, default: the NEMA23-Adapter
venv in the Tercio-s1 repo); the result comes back as ordinary locked tracks and vias.
Freerouting 2.5 left ~180 of ~300 connections unrouted here and knows nothing about gate
loops or Kelvin pairs, so the router is ours.

- 0.05 mm grid on F / In2 / In3 / B (In1 and In4 are solid GND: vias only). Widths and
  clearances per net class, exact distances plus a 0.04 mm margin, so DRC passes by
  construction. Wide classes neck down to 0.2 mm at small pads.
- Via-in-pad is allowed: JLC fills and caps vias in pads on 6-layer boards at no charge.
  Vias are 0.45/0.2 mm for logic, 0.5/0.25 gate, 0.6/0.3 supply, 0.7/0.35 power.
- Power pours are walls for other nets, except right around a connection's own pads.
- Negotiated congestion (PathFinder): every net is routed, overlaps cost more each round
  (present and history), and the nets still in conflict are routed again until nothing
  overlaps. Then legalisation drops the least important net of each remaining conflict and
  puts it back with everyone else's copper as walls; a rescue step rips up lower-priority
  nets that block what is still unrouted; anything left is promoted (within its tier) for
  another pass. Searches that run out of budget get one wider try.
- Pairs: SNx is drawn to SPx (Kelvin), the SHx return to GHx (small gate loop).

The solved routes are stored in `b1/routes.json` (`layout.py --save-routes`) and re-applied on
every regeneration, so the board rebuilds in under a minute and the router only adds what is
missing. After a placement change, move `b1/routes.json` aside and route again.

Plan and tiers (`b1/routing.py`): Kelvin pairs, gate loops, the buck's input loop (VIN → its 1 µF),
DRV supply and buck first; then a GND via per pad; +3V3 (0.25 mm), +3.3VA and the V_BUS feeds;
then the rest, shortest first. Net classes: logic and Kelvin 0.15/0.15 mm (JLC's minimum for 2 oz
outer copper, so no slack there), gate 0.2/0.2, supply 0.3/0.2, power 0.4/0.2.
A run takes about 5–10 minutes. Failures, if any, are listed in `build/route-failures.txt`.

### Silkscreen

`b1/art.py` draws it, in IBM Plex (the website's type) turned into filled polygons by
`gen/fonttext.py` from the WOFF files in `lib/fonts/` (SIL OFL, licence alongside), so nobody needs
the font installed and the gerbers carry exactly these shapes. Everything goes through a small
planner that keeps each item off pads, part bodies and footprint outlines, holes, the edge and
the other labels, searching outward from where it belongs; test-point labels must sit closer to
their own pad than to any other test point, and every test point reserves room for its label when
it is placed. Sizes stay at JLC's legend minimums or above (strokes ≥ 0.15 mm).

- Top (seen once the board is on a motor): the Tercio mark and "Tercio B1", phase letters A/B/C,
  CAN / AUX / CAN names at the connectors, "10–52 V" at the XT30, STATUS / FAULT at the LEDs.
- Bottom (bring-up): the identity with "FOC MOTOR DRIVER · 10–52 V · REV A", every test point
  named (VBUS, 5V, 3V3, 3VA, GND, SOA/SOB/SOC, GLA/GLB/GLC, SW, FLT), the SWD pad order, the
  solder jumpers (120R, NTC), CAN and AUX pinout tables, phase letters, an S/N field.

### Fabrication (JLCPCB)

```bash
cd ~/Documents/Projects/Tercio-B1/hardware && /Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/3.9/bin/python3 tools/jlc_fab.py
```

writes `build/jlc/`: `tercio-b1-gerbers.zip` (6 copper layers, mask, paste, silk, outline,
PTH/NPTH Excellon, nothing else), `tercio-b1-bom.csv` (Comment, Designator, Footprint, LCSC),
`tercio-b1-cpl.csv` (Designator, Mid X, Mid Y, Layer, Rotation) and `ORDER.md` (order-form
settings). The gerbers follow JLC's KiCad 9 guide to the letter: X2 and netlist attributes on (JLC's
parser reads the layer functions and the board profile from them; with X2 off the upload came back
with no layers or size), absolute coordinates, Protel extensions, PTH and NPTH separate. BOM and CPL
come from the routed board, so they agree with each other and the gerbers. CPL positions are pad
centres in the gerbers' absolute coordinates; rotation is KiCad's angle (top) or 180° − angle (bottom) plus a per-package
zero-orientation correction from the community table kept with the Fabrication Toolkit plugin
(the same formula as Bouni's kicad-jlcpcb-tools). Packages without an entry are listed in
`ORDER.md` to confirm in JLC's placement preview.

`verify_bom.py` exports the JLC BOM (`build/bom-jlc.csv`) and checks every
LCSC code live against JLCPCB: it must exist, match the value and package,
and have stock for the build. It also reports today's component cost.

## Layout of the code

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `gen/sexpr.py` | S-expression reader/writer for KiCad files |
| `gen/symbols.py` | Loads KiCad 10's symbol libraries (flattens derived symbols), pin geometry, `make_ic` for code-defined symbols |
| `gen/model.py` | Circuit model: sheets, parts, pin→net assignments; every visible pin must be assigned or marked `NC` |
| `gen/writer.py` | Model → `.kicad_sch`/`.kicad_pro`, project symbol library; deterministic UUIDs |
| `gen/footprints.py` | Project footprints (Kelvin 2512 shunt with net-tie sense pads, motor phase pad) |
| `gen/custom.py` | DRV8353RS and MT6835 symbols, from the vendor pin tables |
| `gen/catalog.py` | Footprints and LCSC codes (JLC Basic where possible) |
| `b1/*.py` | The Tercio B1 circuit, one module per sheet, with design notes on each sheet |
| `gen/pcb.py` | Board engine (KiCad Python): netlist → footprints, placement with collision search, zones, vias, tracks |
| `gen/router.py` | Board geometry and clearance rasters for the router (the first, slower router lives on here too) |
| `gen/route_export.py`, `gen/fastroute.py` | Routing problem export (KiCad Python) and the Numba negotiated-congestion router |
| `b1/routing.py` | Routing plan: tiers and order, pairs |
| `tools/route_report.py` | Routing review: per-net length / vias / layers for the critical classes, per-layer copper plots |
| `b1/board.py`, `b1/copper.py`, `b1/art.py` | Placement plan, power copper and planes, silkscreen (planner + design) |
| `gen/fonttext.py`, `lib/fonts/` | IBM Plex outlines (WOFF, SIL OFL) → silkscreen polygons |
| `tools/jlc_fab.py` | JLCPCB gerbers, BOM and CPL with rotation corrections |
| `tools/xt30pw_model.py` | CadQuery model of the XT30PW-M (KiCad 10 ships none) |
| `build.py`, `layout.py`, `verify_bom.py` | Generate and check |
| `kicad/` | Generated KiCad project (open `tercio-b1.kicad_pro` in KiCad 10 to review) |
| `build/` | Netlist, ERC report, PDF, BOM (git-ignored) |

## Review workflow

Open `kicad/tercio-b1.kicad_pro` in KiCad 10 (or read `build/tercio-b1-schematic.pdf`).
Write findings down. Fixes go into `b1/*.py`, and then you regenerate. Edits made
in KiCad are overwritten by the next build, by design ("code owns everything").

## Board (rev A) at a glance

- 46 × 46 mm, 6 layers (2 oz outer / 1 oz inner), 1.6 mm, matte black mask, white silk, ENIG,
  filled and capped vias in pads.
- Top: XT30 on the left edge, three half-bridges across the bottom half (high-side over low-side,
  Kelvin shunt beside each low-side FET, 10 µF 100 V bulk in slots around them), phase pads A/B/C
  on the bottom edge, DRV8353RS centred above the legs with its buck, 22 µF 100 V can, right-angle
  JST-GH connectors with their cable entries at the edges (CAN-in GH-4 top left, aux GH-8 top
  right, CAN-out GH-4 on the right edge), status LEDs.
- Bottom (≤ 2.3 mm parts, faces the motor): MT6835 on the shaft axis (turned 180° so its SPI pins
  face the MCU); the MCU beside the DRV, rotated 180° so its PWM / chip-select pins face the DRV's
  control pins across a 3 mm via lane and its aux pins face the aux connector; CAN transceiver
  between the CAN connectors, LDO, logic passives, SWD pads, test points, input TVS, the Tercio mark.
- Copper: In1 and In4 solid GND; In2 V_BUS under the power half (a finger up to the XT30), a phase
  pour per leg down to its pad, signals and GND fill elsewhere; In3 signals, then +3V3 fill under
  the logic half and GND fill under the power half; per-leg V_BUS / phase / LSRC / GND-return pours
  on top; phase pours on the bottom.
